Is Your Child Asking About Contacts?

Contact Lenses for Kids and Teens: A Guide for Pine Beach Parents

Book AppointmentCall: (732) 978-9306

Sports, dance, a new school year—sooner or later, many kids who wear glasses start asking about contact lenses. Whether contacts make sense has less to do with a birthday and more to do with your child’s eyes, habits and readiness. Here’s what parents in Pine Beach should know before that first conversation with an eye doctor.

Common Worries, Answered Plainly

If you’re hesitant, you’re not alone. Many parents wonder about the same two things: whether contacts are safe for young eyes, and whether their child can really manage putting lenses in and taking them out.

Both are fair questions. Because a contact lens sits right on the eye, safety depends on a proper fitting and on daily habits: clean hands, fresh solution, no water, and no sleeping in lenses unless an eye doctor has prescribed them for overnight wear. Handling takes practice, and some kids get comfortable faster than others. Having a parent nearby for the first few weeks helps.

Signs Your Child May Be Ready

  • They’re motivated to wear contacts and to take care of them.
  • They keep up with daily routines, like brushing their teeth, without constant reminders.
  • They can follow the same steps in order every time: wash hands, handle the lens, clean the case.
  • They’re willing to switch back to glasses when their eyes feel tired or irritated.
  • You’re able to help and check in while they learn.

There’s no single right age. An eye exam, plus a frank talk with your child’s eye doctor, is a good way to decide.

Lenses That Can Do More for Nearsighted Kids

For children who are nearsighted, contact lenses can be part of a plan to slow how quickly the prescription changes. The myopia management options at Academy Vision include soft contact lenses designed specifically for kids, as well as ortho-k lenses that are worn overnight. Dr. Tyler Lesko focuses on pediatrics and myopia management, including orthokeratology and MiSight.

Learn more about myopia management and ortho-k.

What a Contact Lens Fitting Involves

A contact lens exam goes a step beyond a standard eye exam. At Academy Vision, it includes these steps:

  1. We check vision and overall eye health.
  2. We take measurements so the lenses fit properly.
  3. We evaluate the tear film to help keep eyes comfortable.
  4. We fit trial lenses so the wearer can feel how they work.
  5. We make adjustments based on comfort and clarity.

New wearers get step-by-step coaching on inserting and removing lenses and keeping them clean. Call (732) 978-9306 to ask how this would work for your child.

Follow-Up Matters as Kids Grow

A child’s eyes and prescription can shift between birthdays, and a lens that fit well last year may need adjusting. Follow-up visits give the eye doctor a chance to check the fit, look for signs of irritation, and make sure good habits are sticking. Ask how often your child should be seen once they’re wearing contacts.

Why a Proper Fitting Matters

A glasses prescription isn’t a contact lens prescription. Contacts need their own measurements and fitting, since they rest on the surface of the eye. Ordering lenses without a fitting, or buying decorative lenses without a prescription, can put young eyes at risk.

More for Growing Eyes

Anyone who wears contacts should have an up-to-date pair of glasses as a backup, and our kids’ eyewear page has more on frames. You can also explore our children’s eye care or read about contact lens exams.

What age can kids start wearing contact lenses?

There isn’t one set age. Readiness depends on maturity, motivation and hygiene habits as much as on the eyes themselves, and your child’s eye doctor can help you decide whether now is the right time.

Can my child sleep in their contacts?

Only if the lenses are designed and prescribed for overnight wear, as ortho-k lenses are. Sleeping in regular contact lenses raises the risk of eye infections.

Can my child swim in contacts?

Lenses shouldn’t be exposed to water, whether that’s the pool, the ocean or the bathroom tap. Water can carry germs that cause serious eye infections, so ask your eye doctor about a plan for swim days.

What if my child’s eye gets red or sore?

Have your child take the lenses out and call your eye doctor right away. Redness, pain, light sensitivity or blurry vision while wearing contacts shouldn’t be ignored. For sudden vision loss or an eye injury, seek emergency care immediately.

Questions About Contacts for Your Child?

Call us at (732) 978-9306 to ask whether contact lenses could be an option for your child and what the next step would be. We’re glad to talk it through.

Managing Diabetes? Let’s Look After Your Eyes, Too

Eye Exams for People Living with Diabetes in Pine Beach

Book AppointmentCall: (732) 978-9306

Living with diabetes means keeping track of a lot: blood sugar, medicines, meals, and appointments. Your eyes belong on that list, because diabetes can affect them quietly, long before your vision feels any different.

At Academy Vision, routine diabetic eye exams let us monitor those changes and step in if needed. Our optometrists take the time to listen and explain, so you know where your eyes stand.

Diabetic Retinopathy Can Develop Without Warning

Over time, high blood sugar can weaken the tiny blood vessels in the retina, the thin, light-sensing tissue that lines the inside of the eye. Those vessels may leak fluid or blood, and in later stages fragile new ones can grow where they don’t belong. This is diabetic retinopathy, and early on it usually causes no symptoms at all.

Fluid can also build up in the macula, the part of the retina you use for reading and recognizing faces, and blur your central vision. Diabetes raises the risk of cataracts and glaucoma as well. The encouraging part is that changes found early are much easier to manage, which is why a yearly exam matters even when you see well.

What a Yearly Diabetic Eye Exam Gives You

  • A clear view of the retina. A careful exam of the retina can turn up changes long before they affect your sight.
  • Information for your whole care team. Your eye results add to the picture your endocrinologist or family doctor is building, so bring them up at your next appointment.
  • A record that grows each year. Every exam gives the next one something to compare against, which makes small changes easier to spot.

What a Diabetic Eye Exam Involves

A diabetic eye exam centers on the retina. Dilating drops are commonly used to widen the pupil so more of the retina can be seen. They can leave your vision blurry and sensitive to light for several hours, so sunglasses, and possibly a driver, are a good idea.

The retina, including its network of tiny vessels, gets a close look, along with the rest of the eye. We’ll talk through anything we find in plain language and explain what it means for your next steps.

The Same Practice, Year After Year

Diabetic eye care is really a long-term relationship. When the same practice sees you every year, each exam can be measured against the last, and gradual changes are much easier to catch. For decades, we’ve been caring for families throughout the Jersey Shore and building relationships that last well beyond a single visit.

A screening on its own only helps if someone acts on the result. If an exam shows early changes, the next step might be closer monitoring, a conversation with your diabetes care team, or treatment from a retina specialist. What matters is that every finding leads somewhere.

Part of Your Medical Eye Care

Diabetic eye disease is one of the conditions we manage as part of our medical eye care. Because diabetes also raises the risk of glaucoma and cataracts, our pages on glaucoma monitoring and cataract care are worth a read too.

My vision seems fine. Do I still need a diabetic eye exam?

Yes. Diabetic retinopathy often has no symptoms until it’s advanced, and a careful look at the retina is the only way to know. Most people with diabetes are advised to have an eye exam at least once a year, or more often if their eye doctor recommends it.

Will my insurance cover a diabetic eye exam?

That depends on your plan. Vision insurance is typically used for routine care, and medical insurance comes into play when there’s a health-related concern with your eyes. Which one a diabetic eye exam falls under can vary with your coverage and the reason you’re coming in. Our team will help check your benefits and explain what’s covered. You can see the plans we accept on our insurance page, and please bring all of your insurance cards to your appointment.

Can diabetic eye damage be treated?

Often, yes. Keeping your blood sugar, blood pressure, and cholesterol in a healthy range helps protect the retina. When more advanced changes appear, retina specialists can use treatments such as injections or laser to help preserve vision. The earlier changes are found, the more options there are.

What if my vision changes suddenly?

Sudden vision loss, a curtain or shadow over part of your vision, or flashes of light with a sudden shower of new floaters need emergency care immediately. For other changes, like vision that blurs and clears as your blood sugar shifts, call us at (732) 978-9306 so we can talk about what to do next.

Our Services at Academy Vision

  • Medical Eye Care »

    We keep a close eye on conditions such as glaucoma and macular degeneration to protect your vision long-term.

  • Glaucoma Management »

    Glaucoma usually moves slowly and quietly. Most people feel completely normal while it develops, and the earliest changes only show up during a careful eye exam.

  • Cataract Co-Management »

    Cataracts usually arrive slowly. Colors look a little faded, headlights start to glare, and reading needs more light than it used to.

  • Dry Eye Treatment »

    If your eyes feel irritated, tired, or constantly dry, we’ll find the cause and help you get real relief.

  • Macular Degeneration »

    The macula is a small area at the center of the retina with a big job. It gives you the sharp, detailed vision you use to read, drive, thread a needle, and pick out a friend’s face across a room.

  • LASIK Co-Management »

    Thinking about LASIK? We’ll guide you through the process and handle your care before and after.

When Your Eyes Can’t Wait

Urgent Eye Care in Pine Beach When Something Feels Wrong

Book AppointmentCall: (732) 978-9306

Eyes don’t pick convenient moments to act up. A branch catches you while you’re trimming the hedges, one eye turns red and sore overnight, or a blur shows up that wasn’t there yesterday. When something feels wrong, it’s worth having it checked sooner rather than later. If your vision shifts without warning, your eye is injured, or redness or discomfort won’t ease up, we offer emergency eye care in Pine Beach whenever possible.

When to Skip the Call and Go to the ER

A few eye problems need hospital care immediately. Go to the nearest emergency room, or call 911, if you have:

  • Sudden vision loss in one or both eyes, even if it seems to come back
  • A chemical splash—rinse the eye with plenty of clean, lukewarm water right away and keep rinsing while someone calls for help
  • A penetrating injury, where something has cut or punctured the eye or is lodged in it—leave it in place and don’t press on the eye
  • A curtain or shadow creeping across part of your vision
  • Flashes of light with a sudden shower of new floaters

Four Urgent Eye Problems and What They Can Mean

Pink Eye

Red, itchy or weepy eyes with crusty lashes in the morning often point to conjunctivitis. Some kinds spread easily, so it helps to know which one you have. Read about pink eye and conjunctivitis

Something in Your Eye

Sand, sawdust or a stray lash can leave an eye stinging and watering. Try not to rub it, and never dig at it with anything sharp. What to do when something gets in your eye

A Red, Sore Eye

Redness can come from dryness, allergies, an infection or an injury. Pain, light sensitivity or blurry vision make it more urgent. What a red eye can mean

Flashes and Floaters

New specks drifting through your vision or flickers of light at the edges can mean the retina needs a prompt look. When flashes and floaters are urgent

Why an Eyewear Store Isn’t the Place for an Urgent Problem

A shop that mostly sells glasses and contacts is set up to fit eyewear. It usually isn’t equipped to examine a hurt or infected eye, treat it, or check how it’s healing a few days later. Urgent care centers and emergency rooms do important work, but they don’t always have the magnifying instruments eye doctors use to look closely at the surface and the inside of the eye. For an urgent eye problem that isn’t on the list above, an eye doctor’s office is the place to start.

You’ll find us right on Route 9 near the Mizzen Avenue light. It’s a standalone building with its own parking lot, so you won’t have to hunt for a spot when your eye is bothering you, and there’s a ramp for patients who need it.

Start With a Phone Call

If your eye can’t wait for a routine visit, please call us at (732) 978-9306 rather than booking online, so we can hear what’s going on.

It helps to have a few details ready: what happened, when it started, which eye is affected and whether your vision has changed. If you wear contact lenses, mention it and bring your glasses along. For any of the warning signs above, skip the call and go straight to the nearest emergency room, day or night.

Insurance for Urgent Eye Visits

Insurance for an urgent visit can feel confusing, but it doesn’t have to be. Medical insurance comes into play when there’s a health-related concern with your eyes, such as an infection or vision that changes suddenly. Our team can help you check your benefits and explain what’s covered, and you can see the plans we accept on our insurance page.
